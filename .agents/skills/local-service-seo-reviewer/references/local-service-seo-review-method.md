# Local service SEO review method

## Purpose

Review SEO as a **searcher-intent and business-outcome problem**, not a checklist or a keyword-density exercise. The objective is to attract *relevant local searchers* who might genuinely consider a session or appointment, and then help them recognize that this provider may suit them. Measure qualified organic inquiries and bookings where possible, not traffic alone.

Read the current project's requirements, source, deployed experience and available marketing context. Treat historical plans as intent, not evidence of current behaviour. Verify factual conflicts before recommending changes.

## Ask these questions before recommending changes

1. **What is the searcher trying to achieve?** Separate exploratory queries (for example, "what does this service involve?"), local service evaluation, price research and occasion-specific needs. Use the actual customer language and location of the project being reviewed. Do not assume all traffic has booking intent.
2. **Does the search result set the right expectation?** Consider title, description/snippet, page relevance, city/service area, and whether a searcher who clicks will recognize the offer immediately. Treat actual Google snippets and SERPs as observed only when checked.
3. **Can the search engine reach and understand the page?** Distinguish discovery, crawling, indexing, canonicalization, structured data validity, and ranking. A page being technically indexable says nothing by itself about ranking.
4. **Is the page genuinely useful?** Does it answer pre-booking questions naturally, with accurate details about experience, pricing, privacy, preparation and location? Avoid irrelevant word-count targets and contrived keyword repetition.
5. **Does the site signal real local relevance?** Confirm actual operating locations, the customer catchment and service area instead of manufacturing location pages, addresses or Google Business Profile claims.
6. **Is there a compelling reason to trust this business?** Credibility comes from factual identity, actual work, clear policies and an intelligible service. Do not fabricate testimonials, reviews, credentials or client results.
7. **Are the images discoverable without compromising the photography?** Evaluate descriptive filenames, relevant nearby text, useful alt text, appropriate sizes/formats, mobile performance, and permission to publish. Alt text is for accessibility first.
8. **What would count as success?** Separate search impressions, organic clicks, relevant sessions, inquiries, bookings and sales. With low traffic, refrain from inferring outcomes or claiming statistical proof.

## Model the SEO journey as distinct gates

`Query intent → result visibility → meaningful click → relevant landing experience → inquiry → booking`.

A failure at one gate cannot be diagnosed using evidence from another. If impressions are near zero, a contact-button redesign will not fix discoverability. If there are relevant search clicks but no inquiries, investigate content/visitor fit, conversion psychology and technical contact reliability before blaming ranking. If source data is not available, mark the gate **unknown**.

## Priority areas for a local service business

- **Search eligibility and technical correctness:** indexability, robots, sitemap, canonicals, response status, mobile rendering, structured data, performance, and media loading. Validate in live/deployed HTML and relevant official tools, not code alone.
- **Intent alignment:** organic landing-page message, page titles and headings, natural wording in the actual customer languages and distinctions between service, informational and price-seeking queries.
- **Real local relevance:** factual geography, clear studio/service-area information, coherent business identity; never generate near-duplicate city doorway pages.
- **Image and video SEO:** avoid sacrificing first impressions to satisfy a simplistic speed score. Preserve editorial quality while making media accessible, appropriately sized and discoverable.
- **Site architecture:** each future page needs a distinct and useful user purpose, genuine supporting information and sensible internal links. Do not create pages purely to target spelling variants.
- **Measurement:** use Google Search Console when supplied; distinguish verified observations from assumptions. Do not invent search volume, rankings, click-through rates or likely gains.

## What not to do

- Do not make SEO copy visible at the expense of the current business's identity.
- Do not use outdated "SEO tricks": keyword stuffing, hidden text, fabricated reviews, automated doorway pages, city names without service relevance, or excessive exact-match anchors.
- Do not treat a Lighthouse score as equivalent to SEO success or a schema type as a guarantee of a rich result.
- Do not assume that "more visitors" automatically means more qualified inquiries.
- Do not change pages, content or metadata just to show activity. Diagnose first and explain potential tradeoffs.
- Do not confuse the original proposed page copy with the site's latest content.

## Evidence hierarchy

Label every meaningful claim:

- **Observed** — verified in live HTML, source code, Search Console data, test output or a demonstrated SERP.
- **Project requirement** — explicitly stated in the current project instructions or verified context.
- **Guideline** — grounded in current Google Search Central documentation or equivalent primary guidance.
- **Hypothesis** — a plausible explanation that still needs validation.
- **Unknown** — not available from current access (e.g. account analytics, actual indexed status, rankings).

When a project requirement disagrees with newer Google guidance or actual verified business facts, explain the conflict before suggesting an update.

## Review procedure and expected answer

1. Clarify the scope: ranking/visibility audit, on-page content review, local SEO, image SEO, technical SEO, site architecture, or combined review.
2. Inspect available current code and, when possible, rendered/deployed experience. Read original project requirements.
3. Trace the SEO journey, identifying *which gate could plausibly explain the observed issue*.
4. Explain the strongest evidence-based findings, competing hypotheses and missing evidence. Prioritize consequences for qualified inquiries.
5. Only when recommendations are requested: propose **minimal, testable changes** that preserve the visual identity and comply with technical constraints; for each, state rationale, expected mechanism and validation method.
6. Only implement changes after requested; do not silently modify SEO/brand content during a review.

Default report sections: **Observed state; search intent & visitor fit; technical discoverability; local relevance; content & media quality; competing explanations; missing data; prioritized next investigations**. Add specific fixes only when asked.
