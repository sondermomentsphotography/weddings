# Research foundations and limits

Read these as **thinking tools**, not ready-made explanations for what the reviewed studio visitors do. Each framework has different origins, methods, and limitations. Use primary or well-established sources when a claim needs support.

## Behavioral design

### BJ Fogg — Behavior Model (B = MAP)

Source: Stanford Behavior Design Lab, <https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model>

Concept: behavior requires motivation, ability, and a prompt to coincide. Reviewer application: consider those dimensions separately for first inquiry, subsequent booking, and purchase. Limitation: this is a high-level explanatory model; the existence of a prompt or a simpler action does not prove an actual conversion lift on this site.

### Behavioural Insights Team — EAST

Source: <https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/>

Concept: Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely. Reviewer application: look for cognitive and practical barriers, relevance, appropriate social validation, and timing. Limitation: a general behavior-change toolkit, not a boudoir-specific scientific conversion rule. The revised version notes that research evolves; treat it as a heuristic.

## Influence and trust

### Robert Cialdini — Principles of Persuasion

Source: <https://cialdini.com/principles>

Concepts include authority, liking, social proof, reciprocity, and commitment/consistency. Reviewer application: identify *authentic* bases for credibility and social reassurance. Limitations: no obligation to apply every principle; fabricated social proof or scarcity is unethical and counterproductive, especially for intimate services.

### Nielsen Norman Group — Trust and Credibility in Ecommerce UX

Source: <https://www.nngroup.com/reports/ecommerce-ux-trust-and-credibility/>

Concept: website trust influences willingness to risk time, money, and personal information. Reviewer application: investigate confidence in craft, business reliability, transparent information, and professional conduct. Limitation: ecommerce findings are informative analogies, not direct evidence about photography clients' emotional safety.

## Agency, autonomy, and decision resistance

### Psychological reactance — research reviews

Sources: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27453805/> and <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x>

Concept: perceived threats to freedom may trigger resistance. Reviewer application: examine how messaging respects agency, discretion, and voluntary participation. Limitation: do not diagnose reactance from nonconversion alone or assume every direct prompt feels coercive.

### Self-determination theory — autonomy, competence, relatedness

Source: <https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/>

Concept: autonomy, competence, and relatedness are central motivational needs in the theory. Reviewer application: ask whether the site supports choice, understandable expectations, and interpersonal respect. Limitation: the theory is not a validated website-conversion scoring rubric and does not predict the motivations of any particular woman.

## UX research and evidence quality

### Nielsen Norman Group — Research for Customer Journey Mapping

Source: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-journey-mapping/>

Concept: journey models should be grounded in interviews, observation, and other actual user evidence, not merely in assumptions about what customers feel. Reviewer application: treat hypothesized emotions as questions to investigate; ask for customer voices when possible.

### Nielsen Norman Group — A/B Testing 101

Source: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ab-testing/>

Concept: low-traffic pages are often unsuitable for statistically reliable A/B tests. Reviewer application: when traffic is low, prioritize technical verification and qualitative understanding over claims of measured experiment wins.

## How to use sources responsibly

1. Name the relevant principle and its hypothesized mechanism.
2. State what is observed on the site and what remains unverified.
3. Explain why the framework may apply **in this context** rather than invoking it by authority.
4. Identify plausible competing explanations and evidence that would distinguish them.
5. Never make up psychological research, invent visitor quotes, or assign numeric effect sizes without direct data.
6. Do not reproduce copyrighted book chapters or copy third-party skills verbatim. This document is an original synthesis.
